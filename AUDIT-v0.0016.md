# LEARNS v0.0016 "Rhythm": audit

**Auditor:** Kehai Interim · **Date:** 2026-11-11 · **Build audited:** commit `083f651` (Argon's delivery, as delivered) · **Spec:** `learns-v0016-rhythm` (Kehai, 2026-11-11)

**Verdict: the instrument is honest, and the headline results reproduce. Four things needed fixing:**
1. The music corpus generator copied its column formatting from the crew test scores, and the report doesn't say so.
2. Tier 3 was a MUST and is only about a third done.
3. Several prose claims go beyond what the tables support.
4. The README's numbers don't match the tables.

I ran the missing ablations. Here's what the full data says:

- **GRU beats LEARNS on music everywhere:** both precisions, unseen genres and crew scores, and every byte role.
- **LEARNS beats a parameter-matched Transformer on music** in both precisions. That's a reversal from prose. The ternary margin is small.
- **Inside LEARNS:**
  - The present helical readout is essential.
  - Retention (the decay) matters a lot.
  - Learned retention is no better than a fixed 0.90.
  - The Givens rotation helps modestly.
  - The partner record R_B still doesn't earn its parameters, on music as on prose.
- **The rhythm hypothesis isn't supported.** No ablation hurts the step column specifically more than pitch or the other bytes.

### The rate-limit seam

The report's interruption log matches how the code runs. Each run is a fresh process that writes its record only after step 1,000 and the final evaluations, and an interrupted job is rerun from step 0. No record spans an interruption. The three bit-identical reruns support this.

Two caveats:
- The batch runners (`run_batch_v16_*.js`) weren't delivered, and records carry no timestamps, so the timeline itself can't be checked independently. The configs in the records are enough to rerun anything.
- To mark reuse, Argon added `reusedFrom: "v0.0015"` to 12 existing v0.0015 records in place. Only that field changed; every measurement and the original `codeHash` are intact. Appending marked copies would have been cleaner, but nothing was relabelled.

## 2. Findings

### 2a. The generator copied the crew scores' formatting (undisclosed)

`exportChannelLoom` picks one of four column-alignment styles per score. Two of them are named and modelled on the **test** scores: "Ed/Vael style" (pad to 7/15/28) and "Kehai peepers style" (pad to 4/16/28). The crew scores were attached as TEST ONLY.

That padding is **19.4% of the crew test bytes** (1,423 of 7,328), and the training corpus taught it. The longest-common-substring rows in Table 1 are exactly these padded columns.

**How much it mattered.** I regenerated the corpora using only the two styles not taken from the crew, and retrained seed 3 of each model:

| Model (ternary, seed 3) | Crew loss, delivered corpus | Crew loss, clean-format corpus |
|---|---|---|
| GRU | 1.1411 | 1.1526 (+0.0115) |
| LEARNS | 1.3425 | 1.3383 (−0.0042) |
| Transformer | 1.4041 | 1.4370 (+0.0329) |

The contamination is real but small, and the ranking doesn't change. Still, crew-set results should be read as "crew music in a partly familiar layout," not fully unseen human style. The unseen-genre set isn't affected: its formatting comes from the generator's own styles.

### 2b. LR sweeps: `tail=1`, uneven grids, one edge

- The sweep records have one validation point each (`valTail` length 1). The benchmark itself uses tail 5.
- The grids differ by model.
- **Transformer float32 picked 0.004, the lowest value on its grid.** I extended it to 0.002 on seed 3: validation 1.0501 against 0.8879. So 0.004 is the real optimum, and LEARNS's float32 win over the Transformer isn't an under-tuning artifact.

### 2c. Tier 3 was a MUST, and was only about a third done (undisclosed)

The spec made Tier 3 a MUST if Tier 2 showed any LEARNS advantage, and Tier 2 shows LEARNS beating the Transformer. The delivery ran:
- `helix=off`, 5 seeds;
- `helixRotation=none`, 5 seeds;
- `helixRetention=fixed`, **1 seed**.

`helixRetention=none`, `frontierPresent=off` and `recordB=off` were never run, and the report doesn't say so. **I ran them:** 19 runs, 5 seeds each, same code and corpus hashes, in `results-audit-v0016.jsonl`. Section 3 has the results.

### 2d. Claims beyond the tables

- **"Rotation helps step bytes nearly 3× more than pitch" and "the exact component that gives LEARNS its victory."** Removing the rotation hurts overall (a LOSS), but its per-role effects on step (+0.024) and pitch (+0.009) are both inconclusive. The only significant role effect is on *other* bytes. With the rotation removed, LEARNS ties the ternary Transformer on unseen genres (inconclusive). It isn't "below" it, and it still beats it on crew scores.
- **`helix=off` reported as a LOSS, CI [+0.0079, +0.3865].** `tables.js`'s own paired statistics give **[−0.0455, +0.4399], inconclusive.** Seed 3 is an outlier (1.45 vs about 1.0), which widens the interval. Removing the helix is worse on all 5 seeds, but by the protocol that's inconclusive. Several prose CIs in the report don't match what `tables.js` computes (the rotation CI also differs), so they weren't produced by the table script.
- **One number in the text doesn't match its table.** "Rotation: *other* bytes +0.0616 (0.7020)" should be +0.0574 (0.6978), as Table 6 shows.
- **The rhythm hypothesis isn't supported by any arm** (section 3). The report doesn't state that verdict.

### 2e. The README doesn't match the tables

- **`helix=off` figures:** the README gives +0.2009 unseen, +0.2422 crew, +0.2194 step and +0.2202 pitch. The tables give +0.1972, +0.2172, +0.2103 and +0.2043.
- **Tier 4 figures:** the README gives lookback 2.2129/2.2136 and B0 2.2016. Table 7 gives 2.2188/2.2228 and 2.2181.
- **"Refuting the v0.0015 walk-order hypothesis":** every Tier 4 comparison is **inconclusive**, so nothing is refuted. "Inconclusive never means equivalent," and it doesn't mean refuted either.

**Reading it:**
- **The present helical readout carries the model.** Without it, loss is about 3× worse.
- **Retention is the helix part that matters.** With retention off (`none`), every byte role gets worse. Fixed retention at 0.90 is as good as learned.
- **The rotation is a modest, real help.**
- **R_B is still dead weight.** It's slightly better off, inconclusive, as on prose in v0.0015.
- **Helix and rhythm aren't specially linked.** Where the helix matters, it matters about equally for step, pitch and other bytes.
