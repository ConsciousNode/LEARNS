### Key Findings from Part 1 Ablations

1. **The Helical Working State + Present Frontier Readout (`helix`, `frontierHelix`, `frontierPresent`, `helixRetention=learned`) Carry the Learning**:
   - Removing `W_present` (`frontierPresent=off`, $\Delta = +0.1363$, `[+0.0402, +0.2272]`), stripping the helical recurrence from the frontier (`frontierHelix=off`, $\Delta = +0.1608$, `[+0.0750, +0.2659]`), disabling the helical recurrence (`helix=off`, $\Delta = +0.1603$, `[+0.0911, +0.2412]`), or removing learned retention decay (`helixRetention=none`, $\Delta = +0.7461$, `[+0.0966, +2.8882]`) worsens test loss on **every single seed (5/5)**. Without the damped helical working state feeding `W_present`, LEARNS degrades to worse than the unigram baseline (`3.16–3.77` vs `3.0953` nats/byte).
2. **Relational Embedding (`relEmbed=on`) Actively Hurts (`relEmbed=off` HELPS on 5/5 Seeds)**:
   - Turning off Relational Embedding (`relEmbed=off`, $e_t = \text{embed}(x_t)$ alone) improves test loss by **$-0.1723$ nats/byte** (`[-0.2322, -0.0817]`), winning on **all 5/5 seeds** (`2.8498` vs `3.0221` nats/byte) while saving 1,024 parameters. Adding $Q(W_{\text{rel}})R_{\text{comb},t-1}$ directly onto the raw byte embedding blurs token identity before it reaches `px_input`.
3. **Most Other Components Are Indistinguishable from NOISE at This Scale**:
   - `coreA=off` ($\Delta = -0.0330$, `[-0.1601, +0.0687]`), `coreB=off` ($\Delta = -0.0156$, `[-0.1742, +0.1165]`), `fpIters=0` ($\Delta = +0.0488$, `[-0.0117, +0.1314]`), `helixRotation=none` ($\Delta = +0.0695$, `[-0.1000, +0.6220]`), and `thermo=off` ($\Delta = +0.0718$, `[-0.0655, +0.1936]`) all cross zero across the 5 seeds (**NOISE**), although `recordA=off` and `recordB=off` suffer severe instability spikes on individual seeds (`max Δ = +5.53` and `+3.22` nats).

### 2. The Dyadic Fixed Point: Does It Converge, and Does Converging Help or Hurt?

We ran the full $5 \times 2$ grid of `fpIters ∈ {0, 1, 2, 6, 12}` $\times$ `fpInject ∈ {off, on}` across all 5 seeds (`3, 7, 11, 19, 23`):

| `fpIters` | `fpInject=off` Mean Test Loss | `fpInject=off` Final Residual $\|K^{(M)}-K^{(M-1)}\|_2$ | `fpInject=off` $\|K_A-K_B\|_2$ | `fpInject=on` Mean Test Loss | `fpInject=on` Final Residual $\|K^{(M)}-K^{(M-1)}\|_2$ | `fpInject=on` $\|K_A-K_B\|_2$ |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **0** | 3.070904 | `0.000000` | 3.813208 | 3.070904 | `0.000000` | 3.813208 |
| **1** | 3.101384 | `6.643586` | 4.943705 | 3.165353 | `5.495186` | 5.037810 |
| **2** | 3.078660 | `7.701604` | 6.054032 | 3.172954 | `4.140861` | 5.093836 |
| **6** | **3.022147** | `6.368816` | 6.971546 | 3.814116 | `1.457042` | 5.004053 |
| **12** | 3.026428 | `3.852507` | 7.488014 | 3.164157 | `1.064784` | 4.941580 |

- **Answer**:
  1. **In practice, the "now" is a truncated iteration, NOT a converged fixed point**: eventhough `W_FA, W_FB` are initialized with spectral norm $\le 0.9$, the `LayerNorm` inside $F_A(K_B) = \text{LN}(\tanh(W_{FA}K_B))$ re-normalizes the vector to norm $\sqrt{d} = \sqrt{32} \approx 5.66$ on every step, destroying contractivity! As a result, `fpItersAvg` is pinned at the cap (`6.000` at `fpIters=6`, `12.000` at `fpIters=12`, never reaching $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), with final step residual `6.37` at 6 iterations and `3.85` at 12 iterations.
  2. **Input injection (`fpInject=on`) significantly reduces the step residual** (`6.37 -> 1.46` at 6 iters, `3.85 -> 1.06` at 12 iters), **but HURTS test loss on every seed at 6 iterations** (`3.8141` vs `3.0221`, $\Delta = +0.7919$, `[+0.0570, +3.4108]`).
  3. **Removing the fixed-point iteration altogether (`fpIters=0`) is statistically NOISE** relative to 6 iterations ($\Delta = +0.0488$, `[-0.0117, +0.1314]`), while running 33% faster.

### 3. $\varphi$-Asymmetric Gate Clip (`phiClip`): Confirmed Inert

- Across all 5 seeds (`3, 7, 11, 19, 23`), `phiClip=on` and `phiClip=off` produce **bit-for-bit identical** training and test losses (`3.022147` vs `3.022147`, $\Delta = 0.000000$ on every seed).
- **Why**: `optimizerStep` first clips the global $L_2$ norm of all gradients combined to `1.0`. Since $|g_{B,i}| \le \|g_B\|_2 \le \|\text{allGrads}\|_2 \le 1.0$, a single element of `W_gB_grad` would have to carry $> (1/\varphi)^2 = 38.2\%$ of the entire network's squared gradient norm to reach `1/phi ≈ 0.61803`. In practice, `max|W_gB_grad|` after global clipping never exceeds `0.05`, so the elementwise $\varphi$-clamp triggers `0` times.

### 4. LayerNorm-Bias Gradient Spikes & `per_group` Clipping

- **Mechanism & Frequency**: Whenever a forward pass starts from `getEmptyState()` (step 1 of training, and $t = \text{len}-1$ of every chunk's `scoutBackwardPass`), `suffix_sig = 0` and `h_prev = 0` produce zero-variance pre-LayerNorm vectors inside `innerFPSolve` and `dyadicFPSolve`. At zero variance, LayerNorm's backward Jacobian scales gradients by `invStd = 1 / sqrt(0 + 1e-5) = 316.23` per iteration. Across 6 unrolled Jacobi iterations, $(\text{invStd} \cdot W_{FA}^\top)^6$ compounds to $10^5\text{--}10^8$ (`maxBetaGrad` reaches $2.26 \times 10^8$ to $8.26 \times 10^8$ across seeds `3..23`). Because `xhat = 0` and `K_in = 0` at the empty state, `grad_gamma` and `grad_W` vanish while `grad_beta = grad * 1` absorbs the full $10^8$ spike. Spikes (`max|beta_grad| > 10`) occur on **2.53% of steps** (`3.8 / 150` steps, concentrated in the early steps before `ln_fp*_beta` moves away from zero).
- **Effect of `clipMode=per_group`**: Isolating each tensor's gradient norm clip (`clipMode=per_group`) improves mean test loss from `3.0221` to `3.0009` ($\Delta = -0.0213$ nats/byte, winning on 3 of 5 seeds, interval `[-0.1857, +0.2242]`, **NOISE** over 150 steps because spikes only freeze ~3.8 out of 150 steps).

## Part 4: One Honest Paragraph

What do LEARNS's six paper-derived components actually contribute on *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*? Only one pathway is demonstrably essential across all five seeds: the damped helical working state (`helix=on` with learned decay `helixRetention=learned`, from *The Drill of Entropy*) read out directly at the frontier via `W_present` (`frontierPresent=on` and `frontierHelix=on`). Removing that fast working-state pathway collapses LEARNS to worse than a unigram table (`3.16–3.77` nats/byte), whereas the slow dual records ($R_A, R_B$) cannot carry character-level prediction on their own. Worse, Relational Embedding (`relEmbed=on`, from *It's All Relative*) actively hurts performance on every single seed: turning it off (`relEmbed=off`) lowers mean test loss from `3.0221` to `2.8498` nats/byte ($\Delta = -0.1723$, winning 5/5 seeds) because adding the record projection directly to the token embedding blurs the current byte's identity. Meanwhile, Component A (`coreA`), Component B (`coreB`), the Dyadic Fixed Point iteration (`fpIters=6` vs `fpIters=0`), Givens rotation (`helixRotation`), `prevrev` record seeding, and the Thermodynamic Hinge Loss (`thermo=hinge` vs `off`) are all statistically indistinguishable from noise across five seeds at this budget, and the dyadic map never actually converges to a fixed point because LayerNorm re-inflates the state norm to $\sqrt{d}$ at every iteration. Against fair, parameter-matched baselines trained in the exact same harness with the same learning-rate sweep, LEARNS ranks **last among neural models** at both 54k parameters (`dim=32`: float32 GRU `2.5211`, ternary GRU `2.8348`, float32 Transformer `2.8591`, ternary Transformer `2.8943`, vs ternary LEARNS `3.0221` and float32 LEARNS `3.1289`) and 168k parameters (`dim=64`: float32 GRU `2.3981`—which beats the Kneser–Ney bigram floor of `2.4349`—and float32 Transformer `2.7819`, vs float32 LEARNS `2.9339` and ternary LEARNS `3.1419`), while running $3\times$ to $6\times$ slower than a GRU.

### Verdict
At this scale and budget, **no LEARNS component beats a plain GRU, and LEARNS as a whole doesn't either.** That's an honest, useful answer, and v0.0012 is the first version that can give one. For that, `run.js` should cover the whole training split (strided or shuffled chunk starts), and the runs need a budget closer to a full pass.
